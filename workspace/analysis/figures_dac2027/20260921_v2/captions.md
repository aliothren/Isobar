# Formal captions (v2)
Written for the paper; no development notes.

## fig1

Fig. 1. The placement phase diagram. Both axes are rates: the device's row-processing rate and the link bandwidth per direction. (a) Intra-die placement: one die's two co-resident operators of the GPT-2-medium class layer over all 70 macro seatings, under the per-operator overlap model, so the regions say whether communication is exposed or masked; the envelope between the placement extrema is 2.67x. (b) Inter-die placement: the 2.7B stack on 544 of 576 dies over the seven named seating rules with ports at twice the link rate, under the serial sum, so the regions say which cost dominates; the envelope is 2.66x. The dotted contour is a 20% placement latency spread. Filled diamonds are published parameters projected onto this instance (HERMES 0.4 GB/s per core-to-core link; ISAAC 4.0 GB/s per inter-tile link and 6.4 GB/s per off-chip link), not measured phase positions of those chips on this workload; hollow diamonds are derived or alternative-mode values projected the same way (HERMES's 4-phase MVM; the M1076's published 2 GB/s I/O capability matched to the model's port capacity, 1.0 GB/s at kappa = 2). The dashed lines are the two bandwidth tiers evaluated in this paper, and the circles joined by a vertical line are those two configurations at the NeuRRAM row rate.

## fig2

Fig. 2. The platform. The workload and hardware configuration enter the mapping, which fixes the logical dependencies; placement and routing at the die and macro levels turn them into per-hand-off link and port loads; latency takes the maximum over resources within a stage and the sum over stages, dynamic energy the routed word-hops. The phase analysis and the deployment search (a dynamic program over block cuts, simulated annealing over die placement, enumeration inside a die) read the same costs; the search feeds placements back. Lower left: one link of the Fig. 5 instance under the compact fill, where 8 flows of 1.05 M words share a link and owe 21.0 ms at 0.4 GB/s, while a consumer's ejection port owes 6.55 ms with ports at twice the link rate.

## fig3

Fig. 3. What the cost model is checked against. (a) The published placement measurements of the Simba package (Shao et al., Fig. 9: one convolution layer, res4a_branch1, on four chiplets with a fixed tiling) against the platform's two estimates for the same five seatings, decoded from the paper into their longest producer-to-consumer distance. Every series is normalized to its own adjacent seating. The busiest-resource bound is nearly flat because the busiest link carries the same load in every seating; the round-serialized estimate, which adds the packet round trips, follows the measured ladder from below (measured 2.53x, estimate 2.40x at the farthest seating). The two are model variants, not a confidence band. (b) An independent instance: a synthetic fan-out batch on a 24x24 die mesh with 17 dies per stage and ports at the link rate, whose 13 candidate placements are scored by the analytical bottleneck cost and by the BookSim packet simulator, each normalized to the same random fill. The dashed line is y = x; the rank correlation, not the line, is the claim. The four search endpoints are 12 to 31% below the reference under BookSim; these are network-batch times, not whole-model gains.

## fig4

Fig. 4. What the deployment choice is worth, and what it costs. Main configuration: 2.7B class, 512-token serial prefill, 544 of 576 dies on a 24x24 mesh, XY routing, 0.4 GB/s per link direction, ports at twice the link rate. (a) Whole-model latency of the evaluated placements, split into the non-communication cost (compute, weight write, digital unit) and the package hand-offs; the ticks under the random and search bars are the five random fills (seeds 0 to 4) and the nine search endpoints. The search bar is the representative layout, the median of three seeds after taking the best of three starts per seed: 9.91% below the best random fill (2118.71 ms; range over seeds 9.66 to 10.12%) and 54.5% below the default stacked fill (4192.54 ms). (b) The same layouts in latency and system dynamic energy (no static term). The three short-path heuristics save about 7.3% of dynamic energy against the best random fill at different latency costs: Least Manhattan +1.8%, NMAP greedy +4.0%, Clustered +20.4%; the search lowers latency by 9.91% and dynamic energy by 0.26%. (c) A different instance: the GPT-2-medium class layer with an attention die and an FFN die, each a 2x4 macro mesh, at 0.4 GB/s; the exhaustive optimum over 280 and 70 macro assignments against the contiguous assignment, with the fixed rest of the layer (21.63 ms incl. two package transfers) included. Energy is not modelled at this level.

## fig5

Fig. 5. Where link stacking comes from, and what the objective is worth. (a)-(c) One ffn hand-off of the 2.7B mapping, five producer dies to five consumer dies, 25 flows of 1.05 M words, placed on a 4x4 die mesh with XY routing, 0.4 GB/s per link direction and ports at twice the link rate; six slots stay empty. Every panel carries the same flows; only the placement differs. Each link direction is a line coloured and widened by the service time it owes; the busiest resource is ringed with its flow count. The compact fill stacks 21.0 ms on one link, the best of five random fills 15.7 ms, and the search 10.5 ms; the port floor is 6.55 ms and never binds here. The search is the same annealer as the main configuration (1200 steps, two starts, three seeds, best over starts per seed, median seed shown). These are hand-off service costs on a controlled instance, not whole-model gains. (d) The main configuration's objective ablation: the same search with only the objective replaced, every endpoint re-scored under the system latency and shown as excess over the best resource-aware endpoint; the distance and hop proxies select layouts 11.4% and 11.2% slower at their best seed.

## fig6

Fig. 6. The conditions that set the value of placement. (a) One fixed pair of layouts, the best random fill and the representative search layout of Fig. 4, re-scored at each bandwidth without a new search. The communication share r of the reference, the communication improvement η of the pair and the whole-model gain G are all percentages, and G = η r holds at every point; the shaded band is the placement-dependent band of Fig. 1(b) for the seven named rules at the NeuRRAM row rate (0.535 to 1.421 GB/s). (b) The search gain against the strongest evaluated baseline of each cell for two package geometries and three port capacities at 0.4 GB/s: per seed the best of three starts, then the median and the range over three seeds. At κ = 1 the port capacity limits the remaining gain; at κ = 2 and 4 the endpoints are link-bound and the two geometries agree within the seed range.
