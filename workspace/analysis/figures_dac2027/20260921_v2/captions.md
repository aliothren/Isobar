Written for the paper; no development notes.

## fig1

Fig. 1. The placement phase diagram. Both axes are rates: the device's row-processing rate and the link bandwidth per direction. (a) Intra-die placement: one die's two co-resident operators of the GPT-2-medium class layer over all 70 macro seatings, under the per-operator overlap model, so the regions say whether communication is exposed or masked; the envelope between the placement extrema is 2.67x. (b) Inter-die placement: the 2.7B stack on 544 of 576 dies over the seven named seating rules with ports at twice the link rate, under the serial sum, so the regions say which cost dominates; the envelope is 2.66x. The dotted contour is a 20% placement latency spread. Filled diamonds are published parameters projected onto this instance (HERMES 0.4 GB/s per core-to-core link; ISAAC 4.0 GB/s per inter-tile link and 6.4 GB/s per off-chip link), not measured phase positions of those chips on this workload; hollow diamonds are derived or alternative-mode values projected the same way (HERMES's 4-phase MVM; the M1076's published 2 GB/s I/O capability matched to the model's port capacity, 1.0 GB/s at kappa = 2). The dashed lines are the two bandwidth tiers evaluated in this paper, and the circles joined by a vertical line are those two configurations at the NeuRRAM row rate.

## fig2

Fig. 2. The platform. (a) The inputs are the workload, the given operator tiling with its tensor dependencies, and the hardware and execution settings; the platform generates no mapping. The evaluation core scores one deployment configuration: it extracts the logical transfers, routes them under the fixed rule and accumulates link and port loads, prices the communication from those loads, and aggregates it with the compute, write and digital costs under the execution relation into latency and dynamic energy. The phase analysis calls the core over device rates, bandwidths and placement sets; the deployment search calls it on candidates and returns the updated deployment. (b) Resource accounting on the controlled instance of Fig. 5: the ffn1 to ffn2 hand-off of the 2.7B mapping, five producer dies to five consumer dies, 25 transfers of 1.05 M words each, placed by the compact fill on the 4x4 die mesh with XY routing, 0.4 GB/s per link direction and ports at twice the link rate; the idle top row is not drawn. Each physical link has one lane per direction, coloured by its cumulative load on a diverging scale whose white is the port bound: idle lanes sit at the zero end, blue lanes cannot bind, red lanes exceed it. The busiest link e* carries 8 transfers, 8.4 M words. Below, the service time of every used link direction in decreasing order on the same colour scale, against the port bound of 6.6 ms: the load concentrates on a few links, the hand-off is link-bound, and the evaluator reads its latency term from e*. The 64.0 M word-hops of the same evaluation are the cumulative traffic that sets the communication part of the dynamic energy.

## fig3

Fig. 3. External checks of the cost model. (a) SIMBA: placement sensitivity. The published Simba measurements (Shao et al., Fig. 9: one convolution layer, res4a_branch1, on four chiplets with a fixed tiling) for five seatings that spread the four chiplets from an adjacent block to the corners of a 6x6 region (insets), against two Isobar readings for the same seatings: the service bound, the busiest-link load of the communication-cost stage in Fig. 2(a), and the execution estimate, the aggregation stage's reading once the packet round trips of the Simba NoP protocol are added. Every series is normalized to its own adjacent seating, so the panel checks the relative change with distance, not absolute latency. The bound is flat because the busiest link carries the same load in every seating; the execution estimate follows the measured ladder from below (2.40x against 2.53x at the farthest seating). (b) BookSim: layout selection. 13 candidate placements of a synthetic fan-out batch on a 24x24 die mesh (17 dies per stage, ports at the link rate), scored by the analytical communication cost and by the BookSim packet simulator, both relative to the same random fill. The inset enlarges the region around that reference: the four search endpoints are 12 to 31% below it under BookSim (27 to 29% analytically), so the improvement the model selects is confirmed by the independent simulator. Among the endpoints the analytical scores lie within 3% of each other while the BookSim times spread over 27%: the model finds better layouts, and ranks the best ones among themselves only coarsely. The rank correlation (Spearman rho = 0.953) is supporting evidence and the dashed y = x a guide; these are network-batch times, not whole-model gains.

## fig4

Fig. 4. What the deployment choice is worth, and what it costs. Main configuration: 2.7B class, 512-token serial prefill, 544 of 576 dies on a 24x24 mesh, XY routing, 0.4 GB/s per link direction, ports at twice the link rate. (a) Whole-model latency of the evaluated placements, split into the non-communication cost (compute, weight write, digital unit) and the package hand-offs. Best random is the strongest of the five random fills (seeds 0 to 4); Search (representative) is the median of three seeds after taking the best of three starts per seed. The inset places all five random fills and all nine search endpoints on one axis: every endpoint is below every random fill; the representative is 9.91% below the best random fill (2118.71 ms; 9.66 to 10.12% over the three seeds) and 54.5% below the default stacked fill (4192.54 ms). (b) The same layouts in latency and system dynamic energy (no static term); the percentages are changes relative to the best random fill. The search lowers latency by 9.91% and dynamic energy by 0.26%; Least Manhattan saves 7.3% of dynamic energy at +1.8% latency against the same reference, which is 13.0% slower than the representative search layout (NMAP greedy and Clustered save about as much energy at +4.0% and +20.4% latency). Against the default fill the search's dynamic energy is +5.1%, so the 54.5% of (a) and the -0.26% here are not the same comparison. (c) An independent intra-die instance, the one Fig. 1(a) refers to: one GPT-2-medium class layer over an attention die and an FFN die, each a 2x4 macro mesh, at 0.4 GB/s; the enumerated optimum over 280 and 70 macro assignments against the contiguous assignment, with the fixed rest of the layer (21.63 ms incl. two package transfers) included, so the 9.02% is the whole-layer gain. Not the 2.7B system of (a) and (b); energy is not modelled at this level.

## fig5

Fig. 5. Where link stacking comes from, and what the objective is worth. (a)-(c) One ffn hand-off of the 2.7B mapping, five producer dies to five consumer dies, 25 flows of 1.05 M words, placed on a 4x4 die mesh with XY routing, 0.4 GB/s per link direction and ports at twice the link rate; six slots stay empty. Every panel carries the same flows; only the placement differs. Each link direction is a line coloured and widened by the service time it owes; the busiest resource is ringed with its flow count. The compact fill stacks 21.0 ms on one link, the best of five random fills 15.7 ms, and the search 10.5 ms; the port floor is 6.55 ms and never binds here. The search is the same annealer as the main configuration (1200 steps, two starts, three seeds, best over starts per seed, median seed shown). These are hand-off service costs on a controlled instance, not whole-model gains. (d) The main configuration's objective ablation: the same search with only the objective replaced, every endpoint re-scored under the system latency and shown as excess over the best resource-aware endpoint; the distance and hop proxies select layouts 11.4% and 11.2% slower at their best seed.

## fig6

Fig. 6. The conditions that set the value of placement. (a) One fixed pair of layouts, the best random fill and the representative search layout of Fig. 4, re-scored at each bandwidth without a new search. The communication share r of the reference, the communication improvement η of the pair and the whole-model gain G are all percentages, and G = η r holds at every point; the shaded band is the placement-dependent band of Fig. 1(b) for the seven named rules at the NeuRRAM row rate (0.535 to 1.421 GB/s). (b) The search gain against the strongest evaluated baseline of each cell for two package geometries and three port capacities at 0.4 GB/s: per seed the best of three starts, then the median and the range over three seeds. At κ = 1 the port capacity limits the remaining gain; at κ = 2 and 4 the endpoints are link-bound and the two geometries agree within the seed range.
